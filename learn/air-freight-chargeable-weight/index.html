Air freight chargeable weight, explained

Airlines charge the higher of the actual weight and the volume weight. Volume weight is L x W x H in cm ÷ 6000. Here is how it works, with an example.

The formula

Airlines charge for space as well as weight. They work out a volume weight and charge whichever is higher, the gross weight or the volume weight:

Volume weight (kg) = length x width x height (cm) ÷ 6000
In inches and pounds: length x width x height (in) ÷ 166.
Chargeable weight = the higher of gross weight and volume weight.

6000 is the IATA standard for air freight (1 m³ counts as 166.7 kg). Couriers and express services usually use 5000 (1 m³ = 200 kg), which makes light, bulky shipments more expensive.

Worked example

One US pallet, 48 x 40 in, built to 60 in high (121.9 x 101.6 x 152.4 cm), weighing 400 kg:

  • Volume: 121.9 x 101.6 x 152.4 = 1,887,790 cm³ (1.89 m³)
  • Volume weight at 6000: 1,887,790 ÷ 6000 = 315 kg
  • Gross weight: 400 kg
  • Chargeable weight: 400 kg, because the volume weight is higher. At 5000 it would be 378 kg.

If the same pallet weighed 600 kg, you would pay for 600 kg: heavy, dense cargo is charged by gross weight.

How to lower the chargeable weight

  • Build pallets lower and fuller: empty space on a pallet is charged like cargo.
  • Use the right cartons: oversized boxes add volume weight.
  • Compare sending loose cartons with palletised cargo; the planner shows both.
  • Check which divisor your forwarder uses before you compare quotes.

Air pallets and containers (ULDs)

On the aircraft, cargo is built up on ULDs: PMC and PAG aluminium pallets (125 x 96 in and 125 x 88 in), or containers such as the AKE (LD3). Each has a contour, a shape the cargo must stay inside; lower deck positions are only about 160 cm high and slope at the edges. The planner checks your pallets against these contours.

Questions

What is chargeable weight in air freight?

The weight the airline charges for: the higher of the actual gross weight and the volume weight (L x W x H in cm divided by 6000).

Why 6000 or 5000?

6000 cm³ per kg is the IATA standard for air cargo. Couriers and express services usually use 5000, which gives a higher volume weight.

How do I calculate volume weight in inches?

Multiply length x width x height in inches and divide by 166 to get pounds (for the 6000 standard), or by 139 for the 5000 divisor.
