Shipping container sizes and inside dimensions

A standard 40 ft container is about 12.03 x 2.35 x 2.39 m inside (68 m³). Here are all the common types side by side.

Drag to turn

Inside sizes decide how much cargo fits; the door opening decides how tall a pallet can be when it goes in. All values are typical: they vary by owner and build year, so check the exact container with your carrier.

Which container type?

  • Standard (dry, GP): the default for boxed goods. 20 ft for heavy or small loads, 40 ft for volume.
  • High cube (HC/HQ): 30 cm taller, so pallets can be stacked two high more often. Usually the best value for light, bulky goods.
  • Reefer: refrigerated. Thick walls make it smaller inside, and cargo must stay below the red load line so cold air can flow.
  • Open top: no fixed roof, loaded by crane from above. For cargo too tall for the doors.
  • Flat rack: no side walls or roof, for machinery and oversized cargo that has to be lashed.

Weight limits

Payload is what the container can legally carry, but road limits are often lower. In the US, about 19,900 kg (44,000 lb) of cargo is a common practical limit for trucking one container. Heavy goods often fill a 20 ft by weight before they fill it by space.

Questions

What are the inside dimensions of a 40 ft container?

About 12.03 x 2.35 x 2.39 m (474 x 93 x 94 in) for a standard 40 ft; a high cube is 2.70 m tall inside.

How many cubic metres is a 20 ft container?

About 33 m³ inside, though about 25 to 28 m³ of boxes is a realistic load.

How tall is a high cube container door?

About 2.58 m (102 in) high and 2.34 m wide.
