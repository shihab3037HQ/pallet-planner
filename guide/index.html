Planner guide

Everything the planner does, and how to read its plans. The numbers it uses are typical values: always confirm the final plan with your forwarder or airline.

Getting started

  1. Open the planner. The first time, pick inches/pounds or centimetres/kilograms (you can switch any time in the sidebar).
  2. A demo project loads so you can look around. Click New to start your own, then Import AWD / FBA upload file on the Shipments tab.
  3. Choose Sea or Air in the sidebar, then set the pallet, limits and container or ULD.
  4. Read the Plan tab first: it shows the pallet count and any problems. Then check the Container or Air · ULD tab, the Pallets tab and Compare.

On a phone, the settings sit behind the Settings button above the tabs.

Import file

The planner reads the same Excel or CSV file you upload to Seller Central. It looks for a header row with these columns (names can vary a little):

ColumnUsed for
Merchant SKU (or SKU)Box labels. If a SKU contains a colour word (for example -Black), its boxes are drawn in that colour
Units per box (or Quantity)Units on each pallet
Number of boxesHow many cartons of this line
Box length / width / heightCarton size. Headings with (cm) are read as centimetres, otherwise inches
Box weightCarton weight. (kg) in the heading means kilograms, otherwise pounds

Boxes are numbered in Amazon's label order (SKU A to Z), so box numbers on the pallet sheets match the printed box labels. Spot-check one label to be sure. An Amazon shipment ID in the file name (for example FBA17ABCDEF) is picked up automatically.

Pallet rules

  • Always on: no overhang, full flat layers, and only the top layer can be part full. A pallet with a part-full top always goes on top of a stack.
  • Height and weight limits default to Amazon's: 52 in per stackable pallet, 100 in for a stack of two, 72 in for a single pallet, 1,500 lb per pallet.
  • Mix box sizes on a pallet: off groups pallets by box size; on uses fewer pallets.
  • Let one pallet carry 2 shipments: only used when a shipment needs more pallets than it has pallet labels.
  • Boxes on their side: can fit more, but check for "This side up" markings.
  • Measure your real empty pallet height. It decides how many layers fit.

The Container tab shows two floor patterns. Option 1 · Straight rows turns every pallet the same way. Option 2 · Two lanes puts one lane long side across and one short side across, which often fits more 40 x 48 in pallets. The option you pick is used for pallet sheets, print and Excel. The Compare tab lists every container and option with the number of containers needed; click a row to use it.

Reading a container plan

  • Floor plan: each spot shows the bottom and top pallet of the stack. Spot numbers run front wall to doors.
  • Fill and balance: volume, floor and payload used, and the weight centre along the length. Once most of the floor is used, aim for 40 to 60%, so the truck chassis and crane lift stay balanced.
  • 3D: drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, click a pallet for its details. Play loading shows the pallets going in, in load order. Camera buttons jump to 3D, top, side or door views.
  • Loading order: front wall to doors, bottom pallet first, then the pallet that goes on top.

Floor-loaded cartons

Switch Load as to Loose cartons to plan without pallets. Cartons are stacked in walls across the container, from the front wall to the doors. Each wall holds one box size of one shipment, so walls stay easy to count and check against box numbers. The plan lists every wall with its box numbers and shows how much floor is left at the doors.

Floor-loaded containers are unloaded by hand. Amazon and many 3PLs charge more for them or ask for pallets, so check with the receiving warehouse. Also check carton strength: the planner shows how much weight sits on a bottom carton.

Air ULDs

Air cargo travels on ULDs (unit load devices): flat aluminium pallets that are built up and netted, or closed containers. The planner places your pallets on the ULD base and checks them against its contour, the outline the cargo must stay inside.

ULDBaseContour heightWhere
PMC / P6P pallet125 x 96 in160 cm lower deck, 244 or 300 cm main deckWide-body lower deck, freighter main deck
PAG / P1P pallet125 x 88 in160 cm or 244 cmLower deck, main deck
PLA half pallet120.5 x 60.4 in160 cmLower deck
AKE / LD3 container61.5 x 60.4 in160 cm, with an overhang on one sideWide-body lower deck
DQF / LD8 container96 x 60.4 in160 cm, overhang both sidesLower deck (767)
ALP / LD11 container125 x 60.4 in160 cm, straight sidesLower deck

Contours slope at the top edges, so a pallet near the edge of the base can be lower than one in the middle. The planner tries two layouts: the most pallets per ULD, and one row down the middle. It uses the one that needs the fewest ULDs with nothing outside the contour. Each spot shows its max height. A pallet that still doesn't fit is drawn in red: lower the height limit, or pick a main-deck ULD.

Edge clearance keeps pallets a little in from the ULD edge for the net and locks. Contours here are simplified and typical; they differ by aircraft type and position.

Chargeable weight

Airlines charge the higher of the gross weight and the volume weight. Volume weight = length x width x height in cm, divided by the divisor:

  • 6000 is the IATA standard for air freight (1 m³ = 166.7 kg).
  • 5000 is common for couriers and express (1 m³ = 200 kg).

The Air tab shows chargeable weight per pallet and per ULD, and what the same boxes would cost to send loose. Forwarders round and add minimums, so treat it as an estimate.

Sea vs air

The Compare tab plans the same boxes both ways with your settings: containers and cubic metres for sea, chargeable weight and ULDs for air. Enter your forwarder's rates (per container and per kg) to see a rough cost. Real quotes add fees, surcharges and minimums.

Print, Excel, share

  • Print / PDF: a summary page, the loading options, one page per container or ULD (with a 3D picture when the 3D box is ticked), and one sheet per pallet with every layer and box number. Choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog.
  • Excel: pallets, boxes, loading plan, loading options and ULD sheets.
  • Save / Open: a project file (.json) you can keep or email.
  • Share link: the whole plan, compressed into the link. Anyone with the link sees the plan, including SKUs and shipment IDs.

Your data

The planner runs entirely in your browser. Shipments are saved in your browser's storage on this device, never on a server. A share link keeps the plan after the # in the link, a part browsers do not send to the server. The page loads two free libraries from public CDNs: three.js for 3D and SheetJS for Excel. Everything else works offline once the page is open.

To see how many people use the planner, the site counts visits with Google Analytics: pages viewed and which features are used (for example "print" or "import"). Shipment data, SKUs and file contents are never sent.

Limits

  • Container sizes, payloads, door heights, ULD contours and maximum weights are typical values.
  • Floor-loaded walls hold one box size of one shipment each; mixed walls are not planned.
  • Air pallets are not stacked. Loose cartons on a ULD are shown as a chargeable-weight comparison only.
  • If anything looks wrong, is still available unchanged.
